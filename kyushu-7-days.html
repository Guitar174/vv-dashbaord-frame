The route — 7 days, Fukuoka out and back

Solid grey = fixed transit (train/subway). Dashed gold = self-driven, the four days with no fixed schedule — pull over wherever the view opens up.

First time in Japan — read this first

A few things this plan assumes you already know:

  • Get an IC card (Suica/ICOCA) on Day 1 at Fukuoka Airport or Hakata Station — saves fumbling with ticket machines and works at convenience stores too.
  • Carry more cash than you think. Yatai stalls, shrine donation boxes, the Takachiho boat, and the Yokagura dance are cash-only — card-heavy Thailand habits won't carry over.
  • Onsen tattoo rules vary by venue. Traditional ryokan baths (Kurokawa's Rotenburo Meguri) can still turn away visible tattoos — check per-bath before you go if this applies to you.
  • Rental car nav is often Japanese-only, and fuel stations split between self-serve and full-serve with different etiquette — ask the counter staff to set English nav before you drive off.
  • Days 3 and 4 both start before 6am with a first-time mountain drive in between (Day 4's is in the dark). If this is your first time driving in Japan, consider dropping the Day 3 dawn shoot to arrive at Day 4's sunrise rested — it's the harder drive and the better photo.

02

Up to Yufuin

HAKATA → DAZAIFU → YUFUIN · TRAIN

  • 09:00

    Dazaifu Tenmangu

    Thirty minutes out of the city. Plum blossom is finishing, early sakura starting, and the red bridge over the pond is the frame everyone comes for.

  • 13:00

    Yufuin no Mori

    A sightseeing train with observation windows, not just transport. Two hours fifteen through the mountains.

    ~¥5,700
  • 15:30

    Yunotsubo street

    Walk the length of it toward the lake. Mt Yufu sits at the end of the road — a clean backdrop if the cloud lifts.

  • 17:45

    Lake Kinrin at sunset

    A warm-up for tomorrow. Small lake, fifteen minutes to walk around, quiet once the day buses leave.

Book ahead

Yufuin no Mori seats sell out weeks in advance. Reserve before you fly, or fall back on the regular Yufu limited express.

03

Mist, then the highland road

YUFUIN → YAMANAMI HIGHWAY → KUROKAWA ONSEN · CAR FROM 10:00

  • 06:00

    Lake Kinrin at dawn

    Hot spring water meeting cold March air puts a layer of mist on the surface. Shoot before the sun clears the ridge, not after — the buildings glow through the fog against a dark sky, and it's a different photo entirely once it's bright.

  • 09:00

    Breakfast

    You're finished before the tour buses arrive at eleven.

  • 10:00

    Collect the rental car

    Passport, Thai licence, and the International Driving Permit. Japan drives on the left, same as home. Ask the counter to set the nav to English before you drive off — it defaults to Japanese-only — and confirm whether the fuel is self-serve or full-serve here.

  • 10:30

    Yamanami Highway

    Open highland road over the Kuju range. No fixed stops — pull over wherever the view opens up.

  • 14:00

    Rotenburo Meguri

    One wooden pass gets you into any three outdoor baths in the village. Cash only, and some baths still turn away visible tattoos — check per-bath if it applies to you.

    ¥1,500
  • 18:00

    Kurokawa after dark

    Wooden bathhouses stacked down a river gorge, lanterns on. Slow shutter, no flash — people are walking to the baths in yukata.

Sleep or shoot?

Today already started at 06:00 and tomorrow's Aso sunrise means another pre-dawn start with a 40-minute mountain drive — your first night drive in Japan if you haven't done it before. If this is your first Japan trip, an early night tonight is worth more than a late lantern shot in Kurokawa.

Kurokawa ryokan · ¥14,000

Car

04

The caldera

KUROKAWA → DAIKANBŌ → MILK ROAD → KUSASENRI · CAR

  • 05:30

    Drive to Daikanbō

    Forty minutes in the dark. Free parking at the top, then a ten-minute paved walk to the lookout.

  • 06:20

    Sunrise over Mt Aso

    The north rim of one of the largest calderas on earth. Spring mornings often fill the basin with unkai — a sea of cloud, with the volcano peaks breaking through it. This is the best shot of the trip. It is also freezing and very windy — colder than "windproof layer" sounds: proper base layer, gloves, a hat, hand warmers, and a spare battery kept warm in an inside pocket.

  • 08:30

    Milk Road

    Ridge road running along the crater rim. Slow drive, frequent stops.

  • 10:00

    Kusasenri

    A grass-floored crater with a shallow pond and horses grazing loose. Wide open and very exposed — the wind does not stop.

    ¥500 parking
  • 13:00

    Kamishikimi Kumanoimasu Shrine

    A forest staircase lined with moss-covered stone lanterns. Shade and soft light — the opposite of the morning, and a useful contrast in the set.

The main crater is often closed

Nakadake vents volcanic gas and access gets shut with no notice — visitors found it closed in March 2026. Don't build the day around it. Daikanbō and Kusasenri stay open, and they're the better photographs regardless.

First mountain drive, in the dark

If this is your first time driving in Japan, the 05:30 drive to Daikanbō is unfamiliar rural road with no shoulder lighting. It's a short 40 minutes and well-signed, but go slow, use headlights on full where there's no oncoming traffic, and leave a few minutes earlier than you think you need. Sunrise timing is somewhat forgiving here — arriving a little late costs you some of the gold light, not the shot entirely.

Stay

Aso or Takamori · ¥8,000

Pack

Windproof layer, gloves, spare battery — cold drains them

05

Down into the gorge

ASO → TAKACHIHO VIA ROUTE 325 · CAR

  • 07:00

    Drive south

    An hour and fifteen on Route 325, out of the caldera and over into Miyazaki.

  • 08:30

    Takachiho Gorge — first boat

    Basalt walls, blue-green water, a waterfall dropping in beside you. Thirty minutes, you row it yourself. Take the earliest slot: it's calm at half eight and swamped by quarter to eleven. Cash only on the day, but reserve the slot online first.

    ~¥2,000
  • 10:00

    The top path

    A kilometre along the rim for the view down onto Manai Falls — the angle you can't get from the boat.

  • 13:00

    Amano Iwato and Amano Yasukawara

    Walk from the shrine down to a river cave stacked with thousands of small stone cairns left by visitors. Dim, and worth a tripod.

  • 20:00

    Yokagura night dance

    Masked folk performance at Takachiho Shrine, running nightly, four centuries old. Low light and constant movement — push the ISO and accept the grain. Cash only at the door.

    ~¥1,000

Castle, then north

  • 11:00

    ¥800

  • 15:30

    Forty minutes for what took you six days to drive around.

    ~¥5,100
  • 18:00

    Momochi Seaside

    Sun going down behind Fukuoka Tower, off the beach. Last golden hour of the trip.

07

Fly home

HAKATA → FUKUOKA AIRPORT

  • 07:00

    Ohori Park

    Sakura reflected in a lake in the middle of the city, empty at dawn. Cheap last frame if the blossom has landed.

  • 10:00

    Hakata Station

    Mentaiko and Hakata dolls, all under the station. No separate trip needed.

  • 12:00

    Airport

    Back on the subway you started on.

Allow extra time at the airport

Domestic and international terminals sit on opposite sides of the runway, linked only by a shuttle bus. Add twenty minutes on top of whatever you'd normally allow.
